What you can write

One household. Many ways to help.

Most life-only agents have one conversation with a family and then leave. You'll have six. That's not a bigger catalog to memorize — it's the difference between selling somebody a policy and actually being their agent.

Final Expense

Covers burial and cremation costs so a family isn't fundraising during the worst week of their life.

Who it's for: seniors, fixed income, simplified issue

Mortgage Protection

Keeps the family in the house after a death. Reframes a payoff into protecting equity and buying time.

Who it's for: homeowners, new mortgages, refis

Term

Large coverage for a defined window — the mortgage years, the kids-at-home years, the income-replacement years.

Who it's for: young families, high need, tight budget

Indexed Universal Life

A death benefit plus a cash value component — growth tied to an index, with protection from the downside.

Who it's for: builders, business owners, tax-aware savers

Annuities

Protects long-term savings from market loss and creates guaranteed income a client can't outlive.

Who it's for: pre-retirees, rollovers, market-loss fears

Health

Medical and supplemental coverage. The line most life-only agents can't write — and the one that opens the door.

Who it's for: self-employed, gap coverage, whole households

If we ever stop being the best place for you to write business, you can walk — and that's the whole point. We'd rather earn you every month than trap you for a year.

02

Comp that works either way

Top contract levels, structured so the producer who never wants a team and the builder who does both make real money. You don't have to recruit to do well here.

03

Yours from the first policy. Not year five, not year seven. Leave the business entirely and they're still yours. No vesting cliff, no fake equity.

The five year path

Year one you have a job you like.
Year five you have something you own.

Most agencies sell you month one. Here's the part nobody shows you — what changes about where your income actually comes from.

Year 1

Producer

You write business. Every dollar comes from a case you personally closed — which is how you learn the products, the objections, and the close for real. Renewals start stacking quietly in the background from your first policy.

    Where the money comes from
  • Personal production
  • Renewals — just beginning
  • Overrides — the day you recruit
Years 2–3

Builder

You're still writing, and now you're bringing on agents and training them the way you were trained. Overrides start carrying real weight. For the first time your income isn't a straight line out of your own calendar.

    Where the money comes from
  • Personal production
  • Renewals — compounding
  • Overrides — building
Years 4–5

Owner

The agency produces whether or not you're on a call today. Renewals compound, overrides carry, and you hold something with transferable value — a book and a team that exist independently of your calendar.

    Where the money comes from
  • Personal production — by choice
  • Renewals
  • Overrides
